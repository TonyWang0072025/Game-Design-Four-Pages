## Method
Vision gives a distant direction in a sentence or paragraph. Genre and differentiating features help; experimental games may start from a feeling or experience. Revisit at milestones to notice drift.
Pillars are guardrails. Usually 3–5 short phrases with explanations, they guide feature selection and prioritization. Teams need a shared interpretation; revising a pillar requires updating dependent decisions.
Loops explain moment-to-moment and longer play using actions and causal or temporal connections. They also help onboarding, early testers, and checking coverage in detailed specs. Loops can be inferred from existing games more readily than original team intent.
These three documents are useful from the beginning. Their shortness is intentional. Keep history outside their current concise bodies.
Resource flow normally follows the first playable. It describes actual resources and mechanics, with unimplemented parts explicitly marked. Resources may include money, cards, time, or information. Edges express conversion; group related systems to reveal overlap.
Inspect potential feedback, redundant inputs/outputs, isolated chains, central hubs, and bottlenecks in reaching victory/failure resources. None alone proves a flaw; compare with intent and playtesting.

## Repository adaptations
Use explicit evidence status, localize outputs, provide copyable templates, and link to GDDs. Permit an explicitly speculative resource sketch for incomplete projects rather than fabricating a finished economy. Separate saving, publication, PR creation, and merging according to authorization. These are repository workflow choices, not quotations from the speaker.
